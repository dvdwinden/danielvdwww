<h1>Returning to RSS</h1>
<span class="font-sans text-sm">August 20, 2025</span>

The previous version of my website didn't have RSS. When I launched its current version [two months ago](https://www.daniel.pizza/journal/building-home-web-ii/) , I also built in a few RSS feeds. Using Eleventy's [RSS plugin](https://www.11ty.dev/docs/plugins/rss/?ref=daniel.pizza "Eleventy's RSS plugin"), this wasn't too difficult. There's one for my [Links](/links) (which houses links to things I find worth reading, watching, or listening to, along with a bit of commentary) and one for my [Journal](/journal) (where I put my own writing). And, a catch-all feed that combines both (you can find a link to it in the footer).

I've had an _on-again-off-again_ relationship with RSS over the years. Fifteen or so years ago I used [Google Reader †](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Reader "Google Reader") or [Reeder](https://reederapp.com/classic/?ref=daniel.pizza "Reeder") as my main way of reading the web, and couldn't imagine otherwise. Twitter changed that (as did the demise of Google Reader), taking over the role of RSS for me. Everyone moved their ramblings to centralised feeds, away from their sites, and RSS started feeling a little clunky to me.

But here we are, now. Twitter's changed beyond recognition, my account since deleted, and using the centralised social networks has started feeling more and more like wading through the internet's equivalent of a smelly swamp. And—I don't know if this is just the view from my bubble—personal websites seem to be thriving. [2023 (or 2024) was their year](https://matthiasott.com/notes/2024-the-year-of-the-personal-website?ref=daniel.pizza "Matthias Ott on the Year of the Personal Website"). People are (and many never stopped) [blogging](https://peopleandblogs.com/?ref=daniel.pizza "People and Blogs, by Manuel Moreale"). And, with blogging comes RSS. For [privacy reasons](https://pluralistic.net/2024/10/16/keep-it-really-simple-stupid/#read-receipts-are-you-kidding-me-seriously-fuck-that-noise "Cory Doctorow on why you should use RSS"), or to [escape the chaos of your email inbox](https://www.citationneeded.news/curate-with-rss "Molly White on RSS"). Even [Reeder made a comeback](https://reederapp.com/?ref=daniel.pizza).

In my search for a decent RSS reader, my friend [Ico](https://mastodon.social/@ico?ref=daniel.pizza "Ico Davids on Mastodon") recommended [NetNewsWire](https://netnewswire.com/?ref=daniel.pizza "NetNewsWire"). It's free, open source, available on Mac, iPhone and iPad, and has been around for _years_. A month or so into using it, I can recommend it myself as well. It's fast, it's easy to use, and I can get behind their [reasons for building an RSS reader](https://netnewswire.com/why-write-rss-reader.html?ref=daniel.pizza "Why Write an RSS Reader") and [app philosophy](https://netnewswire.com/philosophy.html?ref=daniel.pizza "NetNewsWire's app philosophy"). 

Returning to RSS has brought me a much calmer experience for consuming writing on the web; a calm I'd forgotten exists outside of the walled gardens I've gotten accustomed to. I still scroll Bluesky and read things through there, but it feels nice (and mindful) to be building up a curated feed of voices again. 

If you have any recommendations for feeds I can't miss, [do let me know](https://bsky.app/profile/daniel.pizza "Message me on Bluesky").
